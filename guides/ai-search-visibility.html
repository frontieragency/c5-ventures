How ChatGPT actually decides who to recommend

It's not the same playbook as ranking in Google — some of it overlaps, some of it doesn't. Here's what's actually going on underneath the answer, and what you can check yourself today.

A real shift, still early

More people are asking instead of searching.

A growing share of people are skipping the search results page entirely and just asking an assistant directly — "who's the best [trade] near me." It's still a minority of overall search behavior today, but the share is growing every quarter. And unlike a results page with ten blue links to scroll through, the assistant usually just names one or two businesses. You're either in that answer, or you're invisible to that search entirely — there's no page two to be found on.

What's actually happening underneath

Related to Google, but not the same signals.

Your reviews, and where they live

Assistants cross-reference review platforms, not just a star rating — the actual text of what people say, and how recently they said it.

What your own site actually says

Specific, concrete facts — services offered, area served, how long you've operated, licensing — get cited. Vague marketing copy doesn't give an assistant anything to point to.

Third-party mentions

Directories, local write-ups, even forum threads on places like Reddit can get pulled in and cited as a source, whether or not you had any control over them.

Consistency across all of it

If your name, address, or phone number doesn't match across platforms, that inconsistency itself can read as a signal something's off.

What you can do about it today, free

A few things worth checking yourself, no tool required.

Read your own reviews, don't just count them

What people actually say matters more than the star average — that's what gets pulled into an answer.

Make sure your Google Business Profile is actually complete

It's one of the most heavily cited sources. See the full checklist.

Say the specific thing, not the vague thing

"Family-owned since 1998, licensed and insured, serving Orange County" gives an assistant something to cite. "Quality service you can trust" doesn't.

Just ask it yourself

Open ChatGPT and ask the exact question a real customer would ask. Whatever comes back is genuinely what's happening right now — no guessing required.

Worth being upfront about: this is still a fast-moving, early space, and how much it matters depends on how much your own customers are actually using AI assistants to search. It's not a replacement for ranking well in Google — it's an additional place to show up, and increasingly, one people trust more than a page of ads.
