## Post 05 — AI visibility

> First line is what shows before "more". Everything after it has to earn the tap.

Three weeks ago, ChatGPT had never heard of them.

A glass shop here in Orange County. Ask ChatGPT who to call for a frameless
shower enclosure and their name didn't come up. Not buried, not second page —
absent. Three of their competitors came up by name.

Now they come up first. Three different searches, three different parts of the
business.

Here's why that matters more than it sounds.

Someone looked at nearly 350,000 local businesses and checked how often each one
actually got shown. Google's local map pack surfaced a given business 35.9% of
the time. ChatGPT surfaced it 1.2% of the time. (SOCi, 2026 Local Visibility
Index.)

Same business. Same reviews. Same website. One in three on Google, one in eighty
on ChatGPT.

And the share of people asking an assistant instead of scrolling search results
went from 6% to 45% in a single year. (BrightLocal, 2026 Local Consumer Review
Survey.)

So the channel growing fastest is the one you're almost certainly invisible in.
Worse, it doesn't have a consolation position. Google gives you ten results, a
map and some ads — a determined customer can still find you on page one
somewhere. An assistant gives back two or three names and stops. No page two,
nothing to scroll, no ad you can buy your way onto.

If you're not one of those names, you weren't outbid or outranked. You were never
in the running, and the customer has no idea you exist. That's a call that never
rings, and you never find out it didn't.

These assistants also don't work the way Google does. They pull from reviews,
directories, your own site, sometimes a Reddit thread nobody remembers writing.
If those sources don't clearly say what you do and where you do it, you're
invisible — however good your website looks.

Same job, five suppliers, five times typing out the same sizes and the same
glass spec. Then waiting to see who came back cheapest — and hoping the quotes
were describing the same thing. Because if one shop reads "tempered" and another
reads "laminated," you're not comparing prices. You're comparing guesses.

Now it's one click. Pick the suppliers, the request goes to all of them with

## Notes

**Posting order.** 05 before 06. The AI visibility post is the better cold open —
it ends with an instruction the reader can act on in ten seconds, which is what
earns a follow. The CRM post is what turns a follower into a call, and it lands
better once they already believe you can see something they can't.

**Why the stakes paragraph is in there.** "You don't come up in ChatGPT" sounds
like a vanity problem until the gap has a number on it. 35.9% against 1.2% is the
whole argument in one line — same business, same reviews, same site, one in three
against one in eighty. Paired with adoption going 6% to 45% in a year, it says
the fastest-growing channel is the one you are invisible in.

Both figures are lifted from our own AI visibility page and both are attributed
in the caption. Numbers without a source read as invented, and these are not:
SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index across ~350,000 locations, and BrightLocal's
2026 Local Consumer Review Survey. If either page updates its figures, this
caption has to update with it.

**The ten-second challenge is the hook.** Anyone who runs it and doesn't like the
answer has just qualified themselves — which is why the ask comes immediately
after, while they're still looking at the bad answer. A caption that ends on a
domain makes the reader decide what to do next, and almost nobody does.

**The ask is a DM, not a click.** Per §4 of the plan, Instagram DMs sit outside
TCPA and most of these owners run their own account, so a DM is the lowest-
friction reply and the channel already chosen for outreach. Links in captions
aren't tappable on Instagram anyway.

**"We'll tell you if you're fine" earns the reply.** It turns the ask from a
pitch into a question with two honest outcomes. It costs nothing — anyone who is
genuinely fine was never going to be a client.
